# Naming Titles And Achievements

Use this reference when generating or revising称号, 战功名, 技能名, 标记名, or similar flavor names for 三国杀 generals.

The goal is not "pretty ancient words" by default. Good names should compress a character's life, skill image, and design thesis into a short phrase that can survive explanation.

## Workflow

1. Identify the naming target:称号, 战功名, 技能名, 衍生技名, or mark/card name.
2. List the character's key anchors: deed, temperament, failure, relation, object, quote, place, family language, or play pattern.
3. Choose one naming method below. Do not combine too many methods unless the name remains readable.
4. Check the name against the skill: it should illuminate what the player does or what the character suffers, not merely cite a source.
5. Provide 3-8 candidates with one-line explanations when the user is choosing.

## Naming Methods

Use multi-meaning words when the character has layered motives. A single character can carry several senses: harm/planning/teaching/envy, correction/rebellion, falsehood/fire, or object/beast/omen. Explain the active senses.

Use professional or cultural terminology when the figure belongs to a coherent field.围棋 terms can name rescue, urgent points, closing moves, dangerous mouths, and multi-purpose moves; military tactics can name encirclement, exposed exits, or pressure lines; music, calligraphy, divination, or ritual terms can do the same when they match the person.

Use transformed allusion instead of raw quotation. Derive a new phrase from a poem, classic, anecdote, or idiom, then make it point at the game action. A quoted phrase should earn its place by matching both character and mechanic.

Use contrast when the record is sparse or one-sided. A name may invoke a famous archetype and invert it, showing how this person escapes, repeats, or becomes part of the structure implied by the allusion.

Use homophone or near-sound only when it adds meaning. Sound play should clarify an image or hidden thesis, not become a private joke. If pronunciation matters, include pinyin in the explanation, not necessarily in the final name.

Use "一取/二取/三取" layering for clan or major figures. Let one name simultaneously point to historical event, family language, opponent, and final failure. Keep the final surface phrase elegant enough to stand without the explanation.

Use skill-action names when the mechanic is the core image. A skill name can describe the action itself:蓄/发, 分/承, 递/嬗, 斩/钉, 驳/龙, 失/路. Prefer verbs that the player can feel at the table.

Use title and achievement names to carry culture that would bloat skill text. Put obscure references, poetic framing, and family resonance in称号/战功/台词 explanation instead of overloading the rules.

## Checks

Reject a name if it requires a paragraph before it sounds meaningful. Dense names are fine; opaque names are not.

Avoid merely describing official history with no game image. The name should help the player remember why this skill behaves this way.

Avoid overusing rare characters. One difficult character can be effective; several make the name brittle unless the entire design is built around textual density.

For clan names, check whether the title belongs to this individual or to the family. If it belongs to the family, use it as clan language; if it belongs to the individual, make it bend the family language.
