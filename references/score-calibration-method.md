# Scored Result Calibration

Use this reference when scoring designs, comparing rated entries, or absorbing result tables with reviewer scores. Keep the general design criteria in `design-rating-calibration.md`; this file covers score handling.

## Blind First Pass

When a source pairs concrete designs with author explanations, reviewer comments, or scores, evaluate before reading the external judgments:

1. Read the prompt, relevant character facts, and actual skill text. Defer author explanations and reviewer comments/scores.
2. Use the shared evaluation anchors to record the strongest play line, likely failure cases, severity, and confidence. If the source has a rubric, optionally score each dimension against its stated maximum before seeing others' scores.
3. Then read the author's explanation and external evaluations. Compare them with the blind pass by dimension: missed finding, unsupported concern, severity mismatch, agreement, or unresolved disagreement.
4. Recheck each difference against the actual text, legal rules, and a concrete play scenario. Reviewers' scores and majority agreement are evidence, not authority.
5. Promote a calibration lesson only when the evidence supports it. Keep taste differences or unresolved readings attributed and scoped.

## Scored Result Calibration

When interpreting or comparing numeric results:

1. Keep each rubric, maximum, weight, evaluator's score, and comment. Scores describe that evaluator's judgment, not objective truth.
2. Compare the same criteria. Normalize only when maxima differ; inspect evaluators separately before pooling. Never compare totals across rubrics or average away a rules failure.
3. Contrast higher- and lower-rated designs, linking score gaps to comments and observable play. Separate reviewer observations from the standard inferred.
4. Promote reusable standards to `design-rating-calibration.md` or a relevant heuristic. Keep cohort distributions, entries, prompt limits, and uncertainty in the contest reference.
5. Label each benchmark's sample and confidence. Small or themed sets suggest relative patterns, not universal thresholds, causation, or default weights. Do not apply contest weights to ordinary reviews unless requested.

Without a comparable score set, use the qualitative anchors and do not invent numerical calibration.
